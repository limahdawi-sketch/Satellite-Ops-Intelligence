Formula and symbol guide — how the link result is calculated

1 · FSPL

LFS = 92.45 + 20 log10(fGHz) + 20 log10(dkm)

LFS is free-space spreading loss; frequency is in GHz and slant range in km.

2 · Total path loss

Lpath = LFS + Ladditional

Add the non-free-space losses required by the modeled path once; avoid double-counting.

3 · C/N0

C/N0 = EIRP − Lpath + G/T + 228.6

Output is dB-Hz. EIRP is dBW; G/T is dB/K. Both must describe the same uplink or downlink path.

4 · C/N

C/N = C/N0 − 10 log10(BN)

BN is the equivalent noise bandwidth in Hz. Convert MHz to Hz by multiplying by one million.

5 · Eb/N0

Eb/N0 = C/N0 − 10 log10(Rb)

Rb is information bit rate in bit/s. Its threshold depends on modem, MODCOD and service conditions.

6 · Margins

Mraw = Eb/N0 − (Eb/N0)req
Mres = Mraw − Mimpl − Mfade

Raw margin is above the required threshold. Residual margin subtracts the implementation and fade reserves.

What does “Calculate combined” mean?
(C/X)total = −10 log10 [ Σ 10−(C/X)i/10 ]

This combines independent noise/interference contributions in linear power, after each ratio is expressed against the same wanted carrier reference.

  • xᵢ is each entered component ratio in dB.
  • Convert each ratio to its reciprocal linear contribution, add those contributions, then convert the result back to dB.
  • Two equal 20 dB contributions combine to approximately 16.99 dB, not 40 dB or 20 dB.

Do not combine unrelated ratios with different carrier references, bandwidths or measurement points.

How to read Spectrum Occupancy and Power Budget

1 · Bandwidth budget

Bavailable = Busable − Breserved

Available bandwidth is the usable span after removing the reserved span. In this demo: 36 − 2 = 34 MHz.

2 · Occupied span per carrier

Bocc,i ≈ Rs,i(1 + αi) + Bguard,i

Rs is symbol rate in Msym/s, α is the roll-off factor, and guard is in MHz. The model estimates each carrier span; it does not simulate spectral masks or overlap.

3 · Occupancy, utilization and headroom

UB = 100 × ΣBocc,i/Bavailable
HB = Bavailable − ΣBocc,i

The percentage tells how much of available bandwidth is used; headroom is the remaining MHz. A positive headroom is not automatically sellable capacity.

4 · HPA operating power budget

Pbudget(W) = 10(Psat(dBW) − OBO(dB))/10
P(W) = 10P(dBW)/10 · P(dBW) = 10 log10(P(W)/1 W)

20 dBW = 100 W at saturation. With 3 dB OBO, the simplified operating budget is 10^1.7 ≈ 50.12 W. dBW is an absolute power level referenced to 1 W; do not add dBW values as if they were watts.

5 · Power loading

Pallocated = ΣPi · UP = 100 × Pallocated/Pbudget

Carrier powers are summed in watts and compared with the OBO-adjusted budget, assuming all values use the same reference plane. This does not model amplifier nonlinearity or intermodulation.

6 · Net rate and screening constraint

Raggregate = ΣRnet,i · η = Raggregate/ΣBocc,i

Aggregate rate sums entered net Mbps; η estimates net bit/s/Hz. The primary constraint compares simplified bandwidth and power loading—it is a screening hint, not an optimizer or an operator-approved capacity decision.

ⓘ

Screening only: does not model payload frequency response, intermodulation, spectral regrowth, per-carrier EIRP, OBO-dependent C/N loss or regulatory coordination. MHz available is not automatically sellable capacity.

Rain Fade & Availability: formulas and plain-language guide

1 · Baseline availability

Abase(%) = 100 × (1 − Hbase/8760)

Hbase is the entered annual outage duration in hours. 8,760 hours approximates a 365-day year; this is arithmetic, not a rain model.

2 · Scenario outage hours

Hscenario = Hbase × (1 − r/100)

r is the hypothetical outage reduction percentage entered by the user. The tool assumes that reduction; it does not prove the mitigation achieves it.

3 · Scenario availability and saved hours

Ascenario(%) = 100 × (1 − Hscenario/8760)
Hsaved = Hbase − Hscenario

This compares the baseline outage input with the hypothetical reduced-outage case.

4 · Remaining fade margin

Mremaining = Mlink + Rmitigation − Ffade

Subtract illustrative fade depth from the supplied residual link margin after adding the assumed recovery. Negative means the scenario has crossed below zero margin.

5 · Reading the chart

y = Mlink + Rmitigation − x

The horizontal axis x is illustrative fade depth in dB; the vertical axis y is remaining margin in dB. The dot marks the selected scenario, and the zero line marks the zero-margin crossing.

6 · Input meanings and limitations

Current residual margin is imported only when you press Use current. Fade depth and mitigation recovery are hypothetical dB values. The margin gauge shows the sign of this simplified margin, not an outage probability. The model does not convert rainfall or margin into outage probability; ITU-R P.618 is not implemented.

ⓘ

ITU-R P.618 is not implemented here. A defensible availability prediction requires location, frequency, elevation angle, climate/statistical inputs and system-specific assumptions.

MODEL BOUNDARIES

What is implemented—and what is not

✓ Implemented in this prototype

  • Unit-aware FSPL, C/N₀, C/N, Eb/N₀ and residual margin calculation
  • Reciprocal-linear combining of dB ratios
  • Approximate bandwidth occupancy and output-power budget screening
  • Availability arithmetic from supplied annual outage hours
  • CSV parsing and deterministic trend heuristics

! Not included / requires further engineering

  • ITU-R P.618 Earth-space rain attenuation and availability prediction
  • DVB-S2/S2X MODCOD threshold database or frame-overhead modelling
  • Payload-specific intermodulation, spectral regrowth or OBO-dependent C/N degradation
  • Regulatory coordination, interference certification or live telemetry integrations
  • AI model diagnosis; current triage is deterministic and rule-based

CASE STUDY · TRAINING ONLY

Can this hypothetical 30 Mbps satellite service meet its link target—and what limits capacity?

Imagine you are supporting a satellite capacity-planning team. You must run an initial technical screen, explain the result to a manager, and list the evidence still required before approving a service.

Target net rate30 Mbps
Downlink example12 GHz
Slant range38,000 km
01
FRAME THE QUESTION

Do not start with the result—start with the decision.

Question: does the illustrative downlink have positive residual margin under the entered assumptions, and do the example transponder constraints pass the simplified screening checks?

Working habitSeparate the link-quality question from the bandwidth/power-capacity question. One does not prove the other.
02
READ THE INPUTS

Check units and confirm both ends refer to the same path.

InputExample valueWhy it matters
EIRP50 dBWSatellite transmit level for this downlink example.
Frequency12 GHzUsed in the free-space path-loss estimate.

All values in this teaching case are synthetic. A real assessment needs verified beam EIRP, station G/T, geometry and all applicable loss terms.

03
TRACE THE LINK BUDGET

Every output should be explainable from the previous one.

FSPL = 92.45 + 20 log₁₀(fGHz) + 20 log₁₀(dkm)

With 12 GHz and 38,000 km, free-space loss ≈ 205.63 dB.

Lpath = FSPL + additional losses

205.63 + 2 ≈ 207.63 dB total modeled path loss.

C/N₀ = EIRP − Lpath + G/T + 228.6

50 − 207.63 + 22 + 228.6 ≈ 92.97 dB-Hz.

Eb/N₀ = C/N₀ − 10 log₁₀(Rb)

At 30 Mbps, Eb/N₀ ≈ 18.20 dB.

Mres = Eb/N₀ − threshold − implementation − fade reserve

18.20 − 5 − 1.5 − 3 ≈ 8.70 dB residual margin.

Initial link-screen conclusion+8.70 dBPositive under these entered assumptions—not a certified link budget.
04
SCREEN THE TRANSPONDER

Find the leading constraint—but do not oversell the spare capacity.

In this simplified screen, bandwidth utilization is the leading constraint because it is the more heavily used of the two indicators. The remaining 5.5 MHz is not automatically available for sale: check spectral placement, guard bands, interference, HPA operating conditions and service-specific requirements.

05
STRESS THE SCENARIO

Ask what changes if the fade assumption gets worse.

Mremaining = Mlink + Rmitigation − Ffade

8.7 + 2 − 6 = +4.7 dB in the current illustrative scenario.

8.7 + 2 − 12 = −1.3 dB

If the assumed fade depth rises to 12 dB, the simplified remaining margin crosses below zero.

Availability = 100 × (1 − outage hours / 8760)

8.76 annual outage hours gives 99.9% arithmetic availability. A hypothetical 25% outage reduction gives 6.57 hours and 99.925%.

Important limitFade-margin sensitivity and annual availability arithmetic are separate calculations here. This page does not infer outage probability from fade margin or implement ITU-R P.618.
06
TURN CALCULATIONS INTO ACTION

A good engineer explains the decision and the missing evidence.

  1. State what was assessed: a hypothetical 30 Mbps downlink and a separate example multi-carrier plan.
  2. Show the key calculated results, units, thresholds, reserves and assumptions.
  3. Identify the leading screening constraint and the next measurements/documents needed.
  4. Ask for validated EIRP/G/T, modem/MODCOD threshold, payload limits, spectral plan and measured outage history.
  5. Export a decision brief for technical review; do not treat the output as an operational approval.
